# About Shattak

## Learn anything. Pay only if it was worth it.

Shattak is a learning platform where every course is free to enroll in, built around a real outcome, and paid for only after you finish. You decide the amount. It can be ₹0.

That is the whole model. The rest of this page explains why we built it that way.

## Why we exist

We believe the world gets better one educated person at a time.

That is not idealism. People are the building blocks of society. The more genuinely educated everyone is, the better the decisions they make, the better the communities they build, and the better the world gets to live in.

But governments will never fix education on their own. Not because they don't care, but because it isn't really their job. Their primary task is to keep a country intact, safe, and running. Basic education fits that goal. Quality education doesn't always.

So if we wait for the system to change, we wait forever. We are not interested in waiting.

There is a second thing we are certain about, from watching people learn, fail, improve, and eventually figure things out: people don't learn by watching. They learn by doing. Not by consuming a 40 hour course. Not by collecting certificates. By actually building something, by sitting with a problem long enough to solve it, by having the experience of "I did this myself."

That gap is what Shattak is trying to close.

## What you can learn here

Shattak brings courses on almost any subject or skill into one place.

On the professional side, that means technology, programming, product management, marketing, and business. On the creative side, guitar, music, comedy, and other creative arts. We also cover academic and university subjects: physics, chemistry, mathematics, thermodynamics, engineering, humanities, political science, and many others taught in colleges and universities.

The catalogue keeps growing. The intention is to cover it all.

## Learn first, pay later

Every course on Shattak is free to enroll in. You can start learning, finish the entire course, earn your certificate, and only then decide what to pay.

You can pay ₹0. You can pay ₹100. You can pay ₹1,000. It is completely up to you. There are no mandatory fees, no pressure, and no questions asked if you choose to pay nothing.

The only condition is that before enrolling in your next course, you settle up on the previous one. That amount can still be ₹0.

The thinking behind this is simple. People are more willing to pay after they receive value than before. Learning is hard work already. Whether you want a job, a new skill, a career change, or just a proper understanding of a subject, you are investing time, effort, and consistency. Money should not be the thing that stops you at the door.

So we remove the money barrier at the start, and we trust you at the end. If Shattak helped you learn something real, build a skill, get a job, or move your life forward, we think many of you will want to give something back. Not because you were forced to. Because you decided it was worth it.

## Outcomes, not course completion

Every Shattak course is designed around a clear, measurable outcome.

Depending on the course, that outcome might be a real world project, passing an exam, a portfolio piece, or a specific skill you can demonstrate. Watching the lessons is not enough. You have to apply what you learned and complete the assignment or project attached to the course.

The certificate comes only after you complete that outcome. Finishing videos does not earn it.

## How a course works

You browse the courses on the homepage, open one that interests you, read through the curriculum, and enroll.

After enrolling, you are taken to the course overview page and asked to join the course's WhatsApp mentorship group. The access code you need to unlock the course sits in that group's description. This is deliberate: we want you connected to the people learning alongside you and the mentor guiding the course before you start, not after you get stuck.

Courses are compact and structured. Most have around six or seven modules, each with roughly six to nine chapters, depending on the topic.

The assignment section stays locked while you are still working through the course. Once you complete the required content, it opens, along with bonus material, live session recordings, and extra resources. You do the assignment, submit it through the platform, fill in a short feedback form, and your certificate becomes available.

Then, and only then, you see the option to pay. You can do it right away or months later.

Learn first. Achieve the outcome. Get certified. Pay when you are ready.

## Mentors

Students learn faster when they have access to people who have already done what they are trying to do.

Shattak brings experienced professionals onto the platform as mentors. Someone may qualify through five or more years of relevant professional experience, strong career growth at a reputable company, cracking competitive exams or difficult interviews, or proven success in freelancing and entrepreneurship. What matters is not the number of years but whether they have real world knowledge and results that can actually help a student.

Mentors support students through one on one mentorship, live classes, doubt solving sessions, and guidance inside the community, depending on what students need.

They also help build the courses. Instead of producing generic content, we work with people who know the subject in practice to define what students actually need to learn, the right sequence, the outcomes worth aiming for, and the projects that prove those outcomes.

## Community

The community is a real part of how Shattak works, not a side feature.

Right now we run it on WhatsApp, because that is what students already use and it keeps things simple. Inside the groups, students ask questions, discuss careers and coursework, share resources and opportunities, help each other, and talk to mentors directly.

As we grow, we may move to Telegram or eventually build the community into the Shattak platform itself. For now, WhatsApp lets us build and manage it well without needing serious infrastructure or funding.

## How we build courses today

Shattak currently follows a resource curation model.

A course combines written explanations and learning material, carefully chosen YouTube videos that already explain a topic well, practical assignments and projects, curated external resources, and mentor guidance.

We are open about this. Rather than re create explanations that already exist and are excellent, we find the best available resource for each topic and organize everything into a structured path. The point is not to hand you a playlist. The point is that you always know what to learn next, what to practice, what to build, and what outcome you are working toward.

As we grow and resources allow, we will produce more original video lectures, learning material, and interactive experiences. In this early stage, using strong existing resources lets us put our energy where it matters most: curriculum, mentorship, community, assignments, and whether students actually finish.

## What we are building next

Shattak is meant to become a full learning ecosystem, not a course catalogue.

We are working on live learning sessions, one on one mentorship at scale, tuition and academic support, career and skill guidance, and tools that help students actually finish what they start. Courses will be updated continuously as industry requirements change, as students give feedback, and as subjects evolve.

The biggest problem in online education is not access. It is that people enroll and never finish. So we are building around the full arc: start, learn, practice, track progress, complete, achieve the goal. The platform tracks how you are progressing, spots where you are struggling, and puts support in front of you at that point.

We want Shattak to be a place where students don't just consume courses. They complete them, build skills, solve real problems, and make progress in their careers and their lives.

# How Shattak Works

**Enroll free. Learn. Build something real. Get certified. Then decide what it was worth to you.**

That's the whole thing. No card at signup, no trial that quietly ends, no "unlock the full course" button halfway through.

Below is exactly what happens, step by step, from the moment you open a course page to the moment you decide what to pay.

---

## The short version

| | |
|---|---|
| **1** | Find a course and hit Enroll. Costs nothing. |
| **2** | Join the course WhatsApp group, grab the access code. |
| **3** | Work through the modules. |
| **4** | Finish the content, assignment unlocks. |
| **5** | Build it. Submit it. |
| **6** | Leave feedback, download your certificate. |
| **7** | Pay whatever you think it was worth. Even ₹0. |

Now the long version.

---

## Step 1 — Find a course and enroll

Browse the courses on the homepage. Open one that looks right and read the details page properly: what the course covers, how the curriculum is laid out, and what you'll walk away with.

Then click **Enroll**.

That's it. No payment page. No "starting at ₹X". You're in.

---

## Step 2 — Join the mentorship community

After you enroll, you land on the Course Overview page, and we'll ask you to join the course's WhatsApp group.

Inside the group description, there's a unique access code. Enter that code on the course page and your content unlocks.

Why the extra step instead of dropping you straight into the videos? Because learning alone is the fastest way to quit. The group is where you'll ask the dumb question you're embarrassed to ask, see other people stuck on the same chapter, and get answers from a mentor who has actually done the thing.

We'd rather you be connected before you start than go looking for help after you've already given up.

---

## Step 3 — Start learning

Most Shattak courses run around **6 to 7 modules**, with roughly **6 to 9 chapters** in each. It depends on the subject. Some topics need more room, some need less, and we don't pad a course to make the number look impressive.

Every course is built backwards from one clear outcome. Depending on what you picked, that's:

- a working project you can show people
- a specific skill you can use at work
- deep understanding of a topic you needed to actually get
- the ability to solve real problems in that area
- preparation for an exam or assessment

You're not here to finish videos. You're here to end up somewhere different from where you started.

---

## Step 4 — Unlock the assignment

The Assignment section stays locked while you're learning. It opens once you've completed the course content.

A few other things unlock at the same time:

- bonus material
- recordings from live sessions
- extra resources for going deeper

---

## Step 5 — Do the work and submit it

Now you build the thing, pass the practice test, or complete whatever the course outcome asks for. Then you submit it through the platform.

This part isn't optional, and it's the part most platforms skip.

**Watching every lesson does not get you a certificate on Shattak.** Completing the outcome does. If a certificate can be earned by pressing play and walking away, it's worth nothing to you and nothing to the person reading your resume.

---

## Step 6 — Feedback, then your certificate

Once your assignment is submitted successfully, the Certificate section opens up.

Before you download it, we'll ask you to fill out a short feedback form about your experience. Tell us what worked, what dragged, what confused you, what was missing.

We read these. Courses get rewritten because of them.

Submit the form, and your certificate is yours to download.

---

## Step 7 — Pay what you want, when you want

Here's where Shattak stops looking like every other platform.

After you have your certificate, you'll see a payment option. You decide the number.

**₹0 is a real, valid, no-questions-asked answer.** So is ₹100. So is ₹1,000. Nobody sees a list. Nobody follows up to ask why you chose what you chose.

You also decide the timing. Pay right away, pay next month, pay when you land the job the course helped you get. There's no deadline on it.

The reasoning is simple: you can't judge what something is worth until you've had it. We think people pay more honestly *after* value than before it. So we ask after.

> **Learn first. Achieve the outcome. Get certified. Pay when you're ready.**

---

## The one rule

There's exactly one condition, and we want to be upfront about it rather than bury it in the terms page.

**To enroll in your next course, you need to make a payment on the previous one.**

The amount can be ₹0.

Yes, really. It's a decision, not a fee. We just want you to actively make it before moving on, instead of stacking up half-finished courses the way everyone does with cheap sales and free trials. Choosing ₹0 and clicking through takes ten seconds and costs nothing.

---

## What you get alongside the course

Shattak isn't only a place to store courses. The course is the spine, and these are the things around it.

**Mentors who've actually done it.** We bring on people with real track records: 5+ years in the field, strong career growth at good companies, cracked competitive exams or hard interviews, or built something themselves through freelancing or entrepreneurship. What matters more than the years is whether they have real knowledge and results that can help you.

**Direct access.** Depending on the course and demand, that means one on one mentorship, live classes, Q&A and doubt-solving sessions, or guidance through the community.

**The community itself.** Right now it lives on WhatsApp, because that's where students already are and it takes no effort to use. Ask questions, share resources, argue about approaches, find people at your level. As we grow we may move to something else, or eventually build our own space inside Shattak. For now WhatsApp works and lets us put our energy into the parts that matter more.

**Courses that keep changing.** Curriculum gets updated as industries shift, as feedback comes in, and as better ways to explain something show up.

---

## How our courses are made (the honest answer)

We curate as much as we create, and we're not going to pretend otherwise.

A Shattak course today is usually built from written explanations and learning material, carefully chosen YouTube videos that already explain a concept well, practical assignments and projects, curated outside resources, and mentor guidance on top of all of it.

If a brilliant explanation of a topic already exists and is free, re-recording a worse version of it is a waste of everyone's time. Our job is to find the best available resource for each piece, put them in the right order, and build the assignments, mentorship, and structure around them so you know exactly:

**what to learn → what to practice → what to build → what outcome to achieve**

Mentors help design this. They define what you actually need to learn, in what sequence, and what you should have to build to prove you learned it.

As we grow, more original lectures and interactive material will get made. Early on, using what's already good lets us spend our limited time on curriculum, mentorship, community, and whether students actually finish.

# The Shattak Manifesto

I believe the world gets better one educated person at a time.

That isn't a nice sentiment for a landing page. It's just how it works. People are what a society is made of. The more genuinely educated people are, the better their decisions get, the better their communities get, and the better the place becomes for everyone stuck living in it.

So the question I keep coming back to is simple. Who is actually going to educate everyone?

---

## Nobody is coming to fix this

Governments won't. And I don't say that bitterly, because it isn't really their job.

A government's first task is keeping a country intact, safe, and running. Basic education fits inside that mandate. Quality education often doesn't. Literacy numbers can go up while almost nobody learns anything they can use.

If we sit around waiting for the system to reform itself, we wait forever.

I'm not interested in waiting.

---

## What we believe

### 1. Money should never be the reason someone doesn't start

Learning is already expensive. It costs your time, your attention, your evenings, and a lot of frustration before anything clicks. That's the real price, and every serious learner pays it.

Charging money on top of that, upfront, before anyone has received a single thing, filters out exactly the people who need it most.

So we removed it. Every course on Shattak is free to enroll in. You start today, with nothing in your pocket.

### 2. You should decide what it was worth, after you have it

You cannot judge the value of something you haven't received yet. Nobody can. That's why upfront pricing is mostly a bet on marketing rather than on teaching.

We flipped it. Learn the thing. Build the thing. Get your certificate. Then decide what to pay.

You can pay ₹0. That's a real answer and we mean it.

We're betting that when Shattak actually helps someone get a job, switch careers, or finally understand something they'd given up on, a lot of them will want to give something back. Not because a paywall forced them. Because they want to.

If we're wrong about people, we deserve to find out.

### 3. Nobody learns by watching

This is the one I'm most certain about, from watching people struggle, fail, retry, and eventually get it.

You don't learn by consuming a 40-hour course. You don't learn by collecting certificates. You learn by building something. By sitting with a problem long enough to actually solve it. By having the experience of "I did this myself."

Everything else is entertainment that feels like progress.

### 4. Certificates should mean something or not exist

On Shattak, finishing the videos gets you nothing.

Every course is built around one clear outcome: a project, a skill, a deep understanding, a passed exam. You have to apply what you learned and complete the work. The certificate comes after that, and only after that.

A certificate you can earn by pressing play is a lie you tell your future employer. We'd rather hand out fewer of them.

### 5. Starting is easy, finishing is the actual problem

The internet has more free education than any human could consume in a lifetime, and enrollment isn't the bottleneck. Completion is. People sign up, drop off in week two, and quietly feel worse about themselves.

That's a design problem, not a discipline problem.

So we build for the whole arc: start, learn, practice, track, complete, achieve. We watch where people get stuck and put support exactly there. Finishing is the product.

### 6. The best teacher is someone who's already done it

Not someone who read about it. Someone who built the career, cracked the exam, shipped the product, or made the freelance business work.

Those people know which parts matter and which parts you can skip. They compress years into a conversation. That's why mentorship isn't a premium add-on here. It's part of the thing.

### 7. Learning alone is the fastest way to quit

Everyone hits the wall where they feel stupid and consider stopping. What gets people through it is usually another person, not better content.

So you join a community before you start a course, not after you've already given up.

### 8. Ego has no place in curriculum design

If someone has already explained a concept brilliantly and made it free, re-recording a worse version so we can call it ours is a waste of everybody's time.

We find the best explanation that exists, put it in the right order, and build the structure, assignments, mentorship, and accountability around it. Over time we'll create more of our own. But we'll never make something original just to look original.

---

## What we're actually trying to do

Not build the biggest course library. Not sell subscriptions to people who won't log in again.

We want a place where someone with no money and no connections can learn something real, build something they're proud of, get guidance from a person who's been there, and come out the other side with a skill that changes what their life looks like.

And then pay it forward, if they feel like it.

That's it. That's the whole company.

# Career guides

**Find out what the job actually is, before you spend two years chasing it.**

Most people choose a career from a job title, a salary screenshot, and a LinkedIn post. Then they spend eighteen months learning for a role that turns out to be nothing like what they imagined, or one they'd have been great at if only someone had told them it existed.

These guides exist to fix that.

Each one is written by someone doing the job. Not a researcher, not a content team. Someone who has sat in that chair, been rejected for that role, hired for it, and eventually done it well enough to talk about it.

---

## What's inside every guide

We use the same structure everywhere, so you can compare two careers without decoding two different formats.

### The job on a normal Tuesday

Not the job description. The actual work. What you open in the morning, who you talk to, what takes up your afternoon, what the boring 40% looks like. Every job has a boring 40%.

### Who this suits, and who it doesn't

Some people are miserable in roles they're technically good at. We name the temperament the job asks for, and the kind of person who tends to burn out in it.

### Entry points

How people actually get in. Which routes work without a degree, which ones need one, which ones only work if you're already inside a company. Campus placement, freelance to full time, internal switch, self taught.

### The skill map

What you need on day one to be hireable. What you can learn on the job. What's genuinely optional despite every job posting listing it. We separate these three, because job descriptions never do.

### A realistic timeline

How long it takes to go from zero to interview ready, assuming you have a life. Not the "become a developer in 12 weeks" version.

### What to build

Three or four portfolio projects that make a hiring manager stop scrolling. Plus the projects everyone builds that no longer prove anything.

### How the money works

Levels, progression, and what changes your pay in this field. We describe the shape of it: what juniors, mid level, and senior people are paid for, what makes one person worth more than another. We don't publish specific salary numbers we can't verify, because a wrong number is worse than no number.

### How to grow, and where it ends

Where this role leads in five years. Which ceilings are real. What people switch into when they leave.

### The stuff nobody warns you about

The honest section. Politics, on call rotations, unpaid emotional labour, seasonality, layoffs, whatever applies.

# Community

**Learning alone is the fastest way to quit.**

Almost everyone who drops out of an online course drops out at the same moment: stuck on something, nobody to ask, motivation gone, tab closed. Two weeks later the course is a bookmark you feel guilty about.

The fix isn't better videos. It's having somewhere to say "I don't get this" and getting an answer the same evening.

That's what the Shattak community is.

---

## Where it lives

WhatsApp. One group per course.

We chose it because everyone already has it, nobody needs a new account, and questions get answered in minutes instead of days. A forum nobody opens is worse than a group chat people are already in.

It isn't permanent. As we grow we'll likely move to Telegram, and eventually build community into the platform itself. For now WhatsApp lets us run this properly without infrastructure we can't afford, and our money is better spent on curriculum and mentors.

---

## Getting in

The group isn't separate from the course. It's step two of enrolling.

1. Enroll in a course. Free, always.
2. You land on the course overview page, which has the invite link to that course's WhatsApp group.
3. Join the group. The access key is in the group description.
4. Enter that key on the course page to unlock the content.

So by the time you watch your first lesson, you're already in a room with the mentor and everyone else doing the same course. That's deliberate. People who join the group on day one finish at a very different rate from people who never join.

---

## What actually happens in there

Someone posts an error message at 11pm and gets an answer.

A mentor drops a live session link, or the recording afterwards.

Two people find out they're stuck on the same module and work through it together.

Someone shares their assignment before submitting and asks for a tear down.

Job postings, internships, exam notifications, and the occasional useful resource nobody else had found.

Somebody finishes and posts what they built, which is quietly the thing that makes three other people go back and open their course again.

---

## The mentors are in the room

This is the part that makes it work. Mentors aren't a support ticket queue. They're in the group.

They qualify by having done the thing: years of real experience, a serious track record, cleared the exams, built the businesses. They answer questions, run live sessions and Q&A, and take one on one calls when someone needs more than a message thread.

They also help design the courses, which means the person answering your question usually helped decide what you should be learning in the first place.

---

## Ground rules

Short list. We enforce it.

Ask real questions. "Please guide me" isn't a question. What are you trying to do, what did you try, what happened?

Answer when you can. You learned something last week that someone else is stuck on today.

No spam, no referral links, no promoting your course or your channel.

Nothing off topic that derails the group. Good morning forwards, politics, religion, and unsolicited memes belong somewhere else.

Don't message people privately without asking, especially mentors.

Be decent. People here are beginners on purpose. Anyone making someone feel stupid for asking gets removed.

Don't share paid material you don't have the rights to.

If you'd rather not share your number, you can use a secondary number, or ask us and we'll work out another way to get you the access key. Just tell us before you join rather than after.

# Skill guides

**Day 1, day 30, day 90. What do you actually do?**

Search "how to learn SQL" and you'll get a hundred results that all say the same thing in a different order. None of them tell you what to do on Tuesday evening after work, or how you'll know when you're done.

A skill guide is the opposite of a syllabus. It's a sequence you can start tonight, with a way to check whether it's working.

Most people need both. Pick the career first, then work through the three or four skill guides underneath it.

---

## How a guide is built

### What this skill actually is

A plain description, without the buzzwords. Plus what it isn't, because half the confusion in learning comes from people thinking a skill covers something it doesn't.

### Do you need it?

Some skills are load bearing for a career. Some are nice extras that people learn because a course existed. We say which one this is, and for whom.

### What you need first

Honest prerequisites. If you can't do this skill without basic algebra, we say so on line one instead of letting you discover it in week three.

### The stages

Usually four or five. Each stage has a small set of things to learn, roughly how long it takes, and one thing you build at the end. You don't move on until the thing works.

### The project ladder

This is the core of every guide.

Skills aren't measured in hours watched. They're measured in what you can make. So each guide has a ladder of projects that gets harder in a straight line, from something you can finish in an evening to something that takes a few weekends and would look good in an interview.

If you can build the last thing on the ladder without help, you have the skill. That's the whole test.

### How to know you're actually good

Concrete checkpoints. Not "you should feel confident." Things like: you can debug this without searching, you can explain it to someone else, you can do it under time pressure.

### Where people get stuck

Every skill has three or four places where most learners stall out. We mark them ahead of time and say what to do when you hit one, because knowing a wall is coming makes it much easier to climb.

### What to skip

The parts of the skill that most guides include and most people never use. Skipping things is underrated. Time is the real cost of learning.

### Resources worth your time

A short list. Specific videos, docs, books, and practice sites, with a note on which stage each one belongs to. Not fifty links dumped in a folder.

# Interview preparation

**You can know the answer and still lose the interview.**

That's the part most preparation ignores. People grind through question banks for two months, walk into the room, and fall apart at "so, tell me about yourself." Or they solve the problem correctly in silence and get rejected for not explaining their thinking.

Interviewing is a separate skill from the skill you're being interviewed on. It has to be practised separately, out loud, in front of another person.

That's what this is for.

---

## Where interviews actually go wrong

From mentors who sit on the other side of the table:

You can do the work but can't describe the work. You've built things and you narrate them as a list of tools instead of a set of decisions.

You go quiet when you're thinking. The interviewer can't read your mind, so silence reads as "no idea."

You have no story. Your resume is a set of true facts with no thread connecting them, and you've never said the thread out loud.

You answer the question you rehearsed instead of the question you were asked.

You freeze on follow ups. The first answer was fine. The "why did you do it that way?" is what breaks you.

You've never said any of this to a human being before. The first time you practise should not be the real thing.

---

## What you get here

### Prep tracks

A structured path per role. What to revise, in what order, over four to eight weeks, with a mock interview at the end of each phase. Built by mentors who hire for that role.

### Mock interviews with real practitioners

A live session with someone who conducts interviews for the field you're targeting. They run it like the real one, then break down what worked and what didn't. Blunt feedback, not encouragement.

### Question banks that stay current

Questions people were actually asked recently, grouped by company type and difficulty, with notes on what the interviewer is really testing. Updated as the community reports back.

### Practice partners

The community pairs people up to interview each other. Explaining a concept to a peer who's allowed to say "I don't follow" is one of the fastest ways to find the holes in your own understanding.

### Doubt clearing

Stuck on a concept the night before? Ask in the group. Mentors and other students are in there.

## A prep plan that works

Roughly six weeks, assuming you have a job or classes. Compress it if you have a date already.

**Week 1 · Find out where you stand.** One mock interview before you've prepared anything. It will go badly. That's the point. You now know exactly what to fix instead of guessing.

**Week 2 to 3 · Fix the gaps.** Only the ones the mock exposed. Not everything. Most people over prepare on what they're already good at because it feels nice.

**Week 4 · Build your stories.** Four or five real examples from your work, each one able to survive three follow up questions. This is the single highest return week and almost everyone skips it.

**Week 5 · Mock again, harder.** With a different mentor if you can. Different people probe differently.

**Week 6 · Rehearse, don't cram.** Timing, opening, questions you'll ask them, and the walkthrough of your main project. Learning new material in the final week mostly just makes you anxious.

---

## How a mock interview runs

You book a slot with a mentor in your field and tell them what role you're targeting.

They run 30 to 45 minutes as a real interview, including the awkward follow ups. No hints, no softening.

Then they break it down: what you got right, where you lost them, what your answers signalled that you didn't intend, and what to fix before the real one.

You get notes you can act on. Then you go and do the fixing, and come back.

## FAQ

**I don't have an interview scheduled yet. Should I still prep?**
Yes, and this is the better time to do it. Preparation is much less effective when it's compressed into the week before.

**I have an interview in four days.**
Do one mock immediately, fix only the biggest problem it reveals, and rehearse your project walkthrough. Skip the rest.

**I have no work experience.**
Then your projects are your experience, and how you talk about them is everything. The campus track covers exactly this.

**Can I do mocks in Hindi or another language?**
Depends on mentor availability. Ask in the community and we'll try to match you.

**I keep reaching the final round and getting rejected.**
That's usually a communication or fit problem, not a knowledge problem. A mock with a senior mentor is the fastest way to find out which.
